Intraday Equity Strategy Research — Summary Report

papertrader / tradex-intraday · backtested against 5 years of NSE minute-bar data (2021-03 to 2026-09) · generated 2026-09-25
This report summarizes an overnight-through-morning research effort: 12 intraday equity strategy variants were backtested against roughly 5.5 years of 1-minute candle data across 165 NSE stocks, using a consistent exit rule throughout (0.5% hard stop, profit-lock arms at 3x risk, then a give-back trail) so results are directly comparable. It covers what the key metrics mean, the top-performing stocks for each strategy, and a worked example of what a monthly return might look like on ₹1,00,000 of capital — including the effect of real trading costs, which turn out to matter a great deal.

1. Key terms, explained

Avg R (Average R-multiple)

A trade's outcome measured as a multiple of how much was risked on it, not as a raw price move. If the hard stop is 0.5% of the entry price, that 0.5% distance is "1R" — the unit of risk. A trade that made twice what was risked closed at +2R; a trade that hit its stop closed at -1R.

R = (exit_price − entry_price) / (entry_price × risk_pct) [sign-adjusted for long/short]

Averaging R across many trades gives a single number that's comparable across different stocks and prices — a ₹5 move on a ₹1,000 stock and a ₹50 move on a ₹10,000 stock can be the same "1R" if the risk sizing was equivalent. Avg R is the number that most directly reflects whether a strategy has a real edge — a small positive Avg R across thousands of trades is what "the strategy makes money" looks like numerically.

Win % (Win rate)

The percentage of trades that closed with a profit. On its own this number can mislead — a strategy can have a low win rate and still be profitable if its winners are large relative to its losers (this is exactly the shape found in this research: most strategies here cluster around 35-45% win rate, with losses capped near -1R by the hard stop and winners occasionally running to +2R, +3R or more once a trade clears the profit-lock threshold).

Win % and Avg R answer different questions: Win % asks "how often am I right?", Avg R asks "how much do I make when I average across every outcome, right and wrong?" A strategy's real value comes from Avg R; Win % mainly affects how the experience of trading it feels day to day (a higher win rate makes for fewer, easier-to-tolerate losing streaks).

Avg PnL%

The plain underlying price move, averaged across all trades — e.g. "+0.055%" means the stock moved an average of 0.055% in the trade's favor. This is not capital return; it doesn't account for margin. At 4x MIS leverage, actual capital return is roughly 4× this figure (see the worked example below).

Trades

Sample size. Results based on fewer than ~100-200 trades should be treated cautiously — this research found multiple cases where a strategy looked excellent on a small sample and reverted much closer to average once tested on a larger one (see the caveats section).

2. Monthly profit calculation, ₹1,00,000 invested

Worked using ma_alignment (confirmed) — the strategy with the most consistent edge across this entire research effort — at 4x MIS margin, 0.5% stop. Two versions: the broad pooled average across all 165 stocks, and the single best-performing stock (IRCTC) specifically.

capital_return_per_trade = avg_underlying_pnl% × leverage(4x) trades_per_month = total_trades ÷ months_of_backtest (67 months) monthly_return% = trades_per_month × capital_return_per_trade

This is the single most important finding in this whole report: a backtest showing a "positive" number is not sufficient — it has to be positive by more than the cost of trading it, and most of what was tested here is not.

4. Caveats — read before acting on any of this

  • Costs matter more than most of the edges found here. Round-trip trading cost (~0.047% of notional at ₹4L) is larger than the pooled average edge of most strategies tested. Only strategy/stock combinations with a clearly larger gross edge survive contact with real costs.
  • Small samples inflate results. This report only shows combinations with 100+ trades, but even those can look better than they'll perform going forward — several strategies produced dramatically different top performers when the dataset grew from 6 months to 5 years (e.g. DLF's strong_rally result fell from +1.115 avg R on 31 trades to +0.316 avg R once retested on 119).
  • No true out-of-sample test has been run yet. All of this was backtested and then reported on the same data — the responsible next step is validating the top candidates on a period the strategy wasn't tuned against.
  • Paper-fill assumptions are optimistic. Backtested fills assume execution at the exact modeled price; real slippage (confirmed once already this session, on an LT trade that overshot its stop within one bar) will be somewhat worse in practice.
  • None of this has been validated with real capital. Treat every number here as a research finding, not a trading recommendation.
